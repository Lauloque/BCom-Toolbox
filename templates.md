# Copy another software

**Please avoid asking to copy a proprietary software** That's potential for troubles (see [this post](https://devtalk.blender.org/t/copyright-guidelines-for-devtalk/17331?u=l0lock) for more context on that). Plus it doesn't make a good feature proposal on its own, since it assumes that both community members and developers are already familiar with what you refer to, which you can't expect.

Blender also has its own workflows and design goals, so what works elsewhere might not be a good fit. It’s much more helpful to focus on the actual problem and how Blender could improve to solve it.

The goal is to make your idea clear enough that anyone from any background can understand the need and benefits, and that a developer passing by can quickly assess how feasible it might be without needing to do more research.

Try structuring your proposal like this:

- **What’s the problem, and why does it matter?**  
  `For example: “With many keyframes, the Graph Editor gets too cluttered, which makes it hard to adjust animations efficiently.”`

- **What are current workarounds, and why aren't they enough?**  
  `Mention what you’ve tried; like filters, channel muting, or custom views ; and explain what limitations they have.`

- **What’s your proposal, and how it fits in Blender?**  
  `Describe the feature or change you’re suggesting, how it would work, and how it could fit into Blender’s interface or workflow. Mockups are gold here. For example, as a toggle or a new option in an existing editor.`

# "Make it better" isn't a valid feature proposal

Proposals like "make it better, faster, or use a better algorithm" don’t provide actionable information for developers. They’re already committed to improving Blender's performance and features, but without specifics, such posts offer no guidance on **what** to improve or **how** to do it.

A strong proposal should clearly outline **what is lacking, why it’s an issue, and how it could be improved.** This allows developers to immediately understand the problem and assess if a solution is feasible. Concrete examples definitely help to contextualize the proposal:

- What is slow or inefficient?
- What specific tools, workflows, or processes are problematic?
- How does it impact your projects or goals?

```
EXAMPLES

⸻For example, instead of saying 'faster simulation,' describe a scenario: 'Fluid simulations with high viscosity take excessively long on 4K meshes; optimizing this could save hours during iterations.'
⸻What **exactly** feels unintuitive about Blender’s material workflow? What specific features or changes do you think are needed? Without these details, it’s hard to even understand the problem, let alone determine how to address it.
```

If you can’t pinpoint the issue or solution, it’s better to seek community feedback or provide detailed examples rather than post vague requests.
